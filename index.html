Floss math

A 100-stitch pattern on 14-count fabric is 7 inches wide. On 18-count it is 5 and a half. The count is the unit nobody reads, and it decides the frame, the fabric and the month.

Measure before you cut

The problem it solves

Cross-stitch patterns speak in stitches and fabric speaks in count - stitches per inch - and the shop sells you fabric by the cut. The conversion is one division, but skip it and the piece you planned as a hoop comes out poster-sized, or the fabric you cut leaves no margin to frame.

Floss math runs the three numbers every project needs: the finished size from stitch count and fabric count (plus the fabric to actually cut, margins included), the skeins to buy from total stitches and how you strand your floss, and the grid lines to mark so a 100-by-80 pattern stays countable.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. The 1.4 cm-per-stitch figure, the size names and the dye-lot advice are labeled craft norms - your tension, your stitch style and your framing shop move the real numbers.
